# Intake Questions

Use these questions selectively. Ask only what changes the recommendation, scope, risk, or deliverable format.

## Priority Logic

P0 questions are required unless already answered. P1 questions improve quality but should not block a quick draft. P2 questions are optional and should be asked only when relevant.

### P0 Required

- Who is the client, and what business does the client operate?
- What decision must this proposal support?
- What problem or opportunity triggered the need for advice?
- What deliverable is needed: memo, proposal, PPT outline, PPTX, SOW, roadmap, or business case?
- Who is the audience or approver?

### P1 Recommended

- What outcome would make the engagement successful?
- What scope, timeline, budget, or resource constraints exist?
- What facts, data, interviews, or documents are available?
- What has already been tried, and why was it insufficient?
- Which stakeholders or teams are involved?

### P2 Optional

- Are there required frameworks, brand requirements, or preferred language?
- Does the proposal need pricing, team composition, or contract scope?
- Are there sensitive topics, political constraints, or no-go areas?
- What should be treated as out of scope?

## Quick Mode Intake

When the user asks for a quick draft or says to proceed with limited information, ask at most three questions:

1. What client and business context should the proposal assume?
2. What decision or action should the proposal persuade the client to take?
3. What output do you want now: one-page memo, PPT outline, or actual PPTX?

If the user does not answer, proceed with explicit assumptions and mark facts to validate.

## When To Skip Questions

Skip client and industry questions if the user supplied a company name, project name, or background document.

Skip problem-context questions if the user supplied meeting notes, a brief, RFP, diagnosis, or existing deck.

Skip deliverable-format questions if the user explicitly asks for a memo, outline, PPT, PPTX, SOW, business case, or roadmap.

Skip budget and commercial questions unless pricing, SOW, or investment approval is requested.

Skip detailed diagnosis questions when the user asks for a first directional draft. State assumptions instead.

## Document-Heavy Intake

When the user provides source documents:

- Extract facts first: client context, objectives, constraints, metrics, stakeholders, dates, and stated problems.
- Identify gaps after extraction, not before.
- Ask only the questions that remain unresolved after reading the source material.
- Track source names and page/sheet/slide references when possible.

## Diagnosis Prompts

Use these only when diagnosis is the task:

- What are the visible symptoms?
- What root causes are suspected?
- Which teams, processes, systems, or incentives are involved?
- Where does the problem create financial, operational, customer, compliance, or strategic impact?
- What happens if the client does nothing for 6-12 months?

## Solution Design Prompts

Use these when the user wants a solution or implementation plan:

- Does the client need strategy, operating model, process improvement, technology enablement, organization change, analytics, governance, or implementation support?
- What capabilities must the client build or improve?
- What decisions, data, or access will the consulting team need?
- Which parts must be implemented first to create proof of value?
